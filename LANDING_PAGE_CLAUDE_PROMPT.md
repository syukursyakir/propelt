# Claude Prompt: Professional Redesign For Propelt Landing Page

## Role

You are an elite product designer, frontend engineer, and startup landing page strategist.

I am building **Propelt**, a professional web app for students, fresh graduates, and early-career candidates applying for jobs, starting in Singapore.

I do **not** want a generic AI resume builder landing page.
I do **not** want a purple-gradient SaaS template.
I do **not** want vague AI hype, floating bento cards, generic dashboard cards, or fake-looking product UI.

I want a landing page that feels:

- professional
- premium
- calm
- credible
- polished
- useful
- student-friendly but not childish
- career-focused
- trustworthy
- modern without looking like an AI-generated template

## Design References And Lessons

The page should apply these professional landing page lessons:

### 1. Respect attention

Do not make users decode what the product does.
The first screen should answer:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- What do I get?
- Why should I trust it?

The hero must communicate the product in one clean idea, not five competing ideas.

### 2. One strong message beats many weak claims

Avoid stacking too many badges, labels, buzzwords, pills, and feature claims above the fold.

Use a clean text hierarchy:

1. small context line
2. strong headline
3. short supporting paragraph
4. clear call to action
5. concrete product preview

### 3. Show the product thinking, not a fake dashboard

The page should not show a generic SaaS dashboard mockup.
If there is a product preview, it should reveal what Propelt actually helps users understand:

- what the job wants
- what the candidate brings
- what is strong
- what is missing
- how a resume bullet improves

The preview should feel like a useful career review, not decorative UI.

### 4. Professional does not mean cold

The page should feel serious and trustworthy, but still approachable for students and fresh graduates.
Use warm neutrals, generous spacing, restrained borders, clear typography, and confident copy.

### 5. Avoid generic “AI startup” visual language

Do not use:

- purple-blue gradients
- glowing orbs
- bokeh blobs
- abstract AI waves
- fake chat bubbles everywhere
- generic bento grids
- fake metrics
- meaningless animated cards

This product is about judgment, clarity, and career confidence. The design should reflect that.

### 6. Premium pages use restraint

Use fewer sections, better spacing, clearer copy, and stronger hierarchy.
Do not over-explain every feature.
Make every section earn its place.

### 7. Copy should sound like a career platform, not a prompt wrapper

Avoid making the product feel like “paste resume into AI.”
The product should feel like a structured application workspace.

Bad:

> AI will instantly optimize your resume and beat ATS.

Good:

> Understand what the role is asking for, then shape your real experience around it.

### 8. The landing page should clarify the product

The founder is still refining exactly how to explain Propelt.
The landing page should make the product easier to understand by using concrete language and visible outputs.

Do not hide behind vague terms like “career intelligence platform.”
Explain what the user actually does and receives.

## Product Context

Propelt helps early-career candidates turn their real experience into a clearer, more targeted job application.

Users can:

1. Sign in
2. Complete onboarding
3. Upload a resume PDF/DOCX or paste resume text
4. Save reusable resumes
5. Paste a target job description
6. Answer guided questions
7. Generate a structured application review and tailored resume

The generated output includes:

- Candidate fit analysis
- ATS keyword analysis
- Tailored professional summary
- Tailored resume
- Explanation of changes
- Additional improvement suggestions

The app must preserve truthfulness. It should not fabricate experience, skills, metrics, internships, employers, certifications, or achievements.

## Product Category

Propelt is best described as:

> A guided job application workspace for students, fresh graduates, and early-career candidates.

It is not merely:

- an AI resume builder
- a resume template site
- an ATS score checker
- a chatbot

The product starts with resume tailoring, but the broader promise is helping users understand the role, understand their own evidence, and position themselves honestly.

## What Propelt Actually Gives Users

Propelt gives users:

1. **Clarity on the role**
   - what the job is really asking for
   - responsibilities
   - important keywords
   - hidden expectations

2. **Clarity on themselves**
   - which projects, internships, coursework, or activities matter
   - what strengths are relevant
   - what gaps exist

3. **Positioning**
   - how to frame real experience for a target role
   - what to emphasize
   - what to reduce
   - how to explain transferable skills

4. **Application materials**
   - tailored summary
   - rewritten resume
   - ATS keyword alignment
   - fit analysis

5. **Improvement guidance**
   - what to fix before applying
   - what skills or evidence are missing
   - what to prepare for interviews

The core idea:

> Know what the job wants. Know what you bring. Apply with a resume that connects the two.

Alternative concise positioning lines you may use or improve:

- Turn real experience into a targeted job application.
- Know what to say before you rewrite your resume.
- Apply with a resume shaped around the role.
- A clearer way to tailor graduate job applications.
- Understand the role. Focus your evidence. Apply with clarity.

## Main Competitors

Propelt competes broadly with:

- Rezi
- Teal
- Kickresume
- Enhancv
- Jobscan
- Resume Worded
- Zety
- Resume.io

But Propelt should feel different.

Most resume tools assume users already know what to say.
Propelt should feel like it helps users figure out what matters before rewriting anything.

The differentiator:

> Propelt is not just a resume editor. It is a guided job application workspace for early-career candidates who need help positioning their real experience.

Competitor positioning notes:

- Rezi is strong on ATS optimization and resume scoring.
- Teal is strong on job tracking and application workflow.
- Kickresume and Enhancv are strong on polished resume presentation.
- Jobscan is strong on keyword matching.
- Resume Worded is strong on feedback and scoring.

Propelt should not copy any single competitor.
It should feel like a more guided, truthful, early-career-focused application coach.

The key differentiation:

> Most tools help users rewrite. Propelt helps users decide what is worth saying.

## Design Standards

Follow these standards:

- Avoid generic AI-generated visual tropes.
- Avoid purple/pink/blue gradient SaaS styling.
- Avoid meaningless bento grids.
- Avoid fake statistics unless they are real.
- Avoid text that sounds like a startup template.
- Avoid excessive animations.
- Avoid scroll-jacking.
- Avoid hover-only information.
- Keep layout responsive.
- Text must not overflow on mobile.
- Buttons must look clickable and stable.
- Cards should have restrained radius, around 6–8px.
- Use strong spacing and hierarchy.
- Keep type scale professional.
- Do not use viewport-width font scaling.
- Letter spacing should be normal, not negative.
- Avoid massive hero text if it causes awkward wrapping on mobile.
- Do not put cards inside cards.
- Do not style every section as a floating card.
- Use full-width bands or clean constrained sections.
- Use cards only for product previews, repeated items, or concrete output blocks.
- Use responsive constraints and stable dimensions.
- Make CTA hierarchy obvious.
- Make the page look good at desktop and mobile widths.

## Typography Guidance

Use a professional type hierarchy:

- Brand: small, confident, bold
- Kicker: small uppercase or small label, used sparingly
- Hero headline: large, clear, not gimmicky
- Hero subtitle: readable, not too wide
- Section headings: strong but smaller than hero
- Body text: comfortable line-height, medium contrast
- Labels in preview UI: small, muted, clear

Avoid:

- too many font sizes
- overusing uppercase
- negative letter spacing
- thin low-contrast text
- giant marketing words that do not explain the product
